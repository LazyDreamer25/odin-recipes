Bole

"Port Harcourt Bole.jpg by Margiftasia is licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0

Description

Bole (or Boli) is a popular Nigerian street food consisting of ripe or unripe plantains grilled over an open charcoal fire, traditionally served with roasted peanuts or a spicy palm oil pepper sauce. While the Southwestern version (Yoruba style) focuses on sweet yellow plantains eaten with peanuts, the famous South-South version (Port Harcourt style) features both ripe and unripe plantains paired with a fiery palm oil sauce, roasted fish, and cooked cow skin (kpomo).

Ingredient

  • The Base: 3-4 large ripe or unripe plantains
  • The Fish (Port Harcourt style): 1-2 medium mackerel or croaker fish
  • The Pepper Sauce: \(\frac{1}{2}\) cup palm oil, 2 large bell peppers (tatashe), 2-3 scotch bonnets (ata rodo), 1 large onion, and 2 tablespoons ground crayfish
  • Seasoning: 2 seasoning cubes and salt to taste
  • Traditional Accompaniment: 1 cup roasted peanuts (groundnuts)

Steps

  1. Prep and Grill the Plantains
  2. Peel the plantains. For even cooking, make light, shallow diagonal cuts across the body.
  3. Preheat an outdoor charcoal grill to medium heat, or set an indoor oven grill/broiler to 200°C (390°F).
  4. Place the plantains on the grill rack. Turn them over frequently every 5 to 7 minutes.
  5. Grill for 20 to 25 minutes until the outside is golden-brown (or lightly charred) and the inside is cooked through.2
  6. Season and Grill the Fish (Optional)
  7. Clean the fish thoroughly, make deep slashes on the sides, and pat it dry with paper towels.
  8. Blend a small part of your onions and peppers with a splash of oil, seasoning cubes, and salt.
  9. Coat the fish generously with this marinade, making sure it gets into the slashes.
  10. Grill the fish alongside the plantains for 15 to 20 minutes, turning once, until flaky and cooked.
  11. Make the Spicy Pepper Sauce
  12. Coarsely blend or chop the remaining bell peppers, scotch bonnets, and onions (do not blend smooth).
  13. Heat the palm oil in a pot on medium heat for 2 minutes (do not bleach the oil).
  14. Add sliced onions and fry for 1 minute, then pour in the coarse pepper blend.
  15. Fry for 10 to 15 minutes until the water evaporates, then stir in the crayfish, seasoning cubes, and salt.
  16. Assemble and Serve
  17. Place the hot grilled plantains on a plate, whole or sliced into chunks.
  18. Serve alongside the grilled fish and pour a generous ladle of the hot palm oil pepper sauce over it.
  19. Alternatively, if serving Southwestern style, simply pair the hot, sweet grilled plantains with a handful of crunchy roasted peanuts.
