White soup

Description

White Soup, natively known as Ofe Nsala (Igbo) or Afia Efere (Efik), is a rich, aromatic Nigerian soup made without palm oil, featuring a light-colored broth thickened with pounded yam. It is traditionally cooked with fresh catfish, chicken, or goat meat, and deeply seasoned with local spices like utazi, uziza, and ehuru (calabash nutmeg), creating a fiery, comforting flavor profile. The soup is most commonly served with hot pounded yam.

Ingredient

  • The Thickener: 3-4 medium cubes of raw white yam (or 2-3 tablespoons of potato flakes/yam flour)
  • Proteins: 1 medium fresh catfish (cut into steaks) or 500g chicken/goat meat, and 1 medium smoked fish
  • The Spices: 1 teaspoon ground uziza seeds, 1 crushed ehuru seed (calabash nutmeg), and 1 small piece of uda (negro pepper, optional)
  • The Greens: 4-5 fresh utazi leaves (thinly sliced, for a subtle bitter finish)
  • Flavorings: 3 tablespoons ground crayfish and 2-3 scotch bonnets (ata rodo), blended or crushed
  • Seasoning: 2 seasoning cubes and salt to taste

Steps

  1. Prepare the Thickener
  2. Peel the white yam cubes and boil them in a small pot of water until completely soft.
  3. Transfer the hot yam into a mortar or food processor and pound it until it forms a smooth, stretchy paste with no lumps. Set aside.
  4. Season and Cook the Protein
  5. Clean your catfish steaks thoroughly (wash with hot water, salt, or lemon to remove the slime) or clean your choice of meat.
  6. Place the protein into a large pot, season with the chopped onions, crushed scotch bonnets, seasoning cubes, and salt.
  7. Add a small amount of water and bring to a boil. If using tough meat like goat, cook until tender; if using catfish, simmer gently for about 10 minutes so the fish doesn't break.
  8. Add Spices and Stock
  9. Pour about 3 to 4 cups of water into the pot (or adjust based on your preferred soup volume).
  10. Add the ground crayfish, smoked fish, ground uziza seeds, and crushed ehuru.
  11. Let the mixture boil vigorously for 5 minutes to fully integrate the local spice flavors.
  12. Thicken the Soup
  13. Cut the pounded yam paste into small, golf-ball-sized lumps and drop them directly into the boiling soup.
  14. Cover the pot tightly and cook on medium-high heat. The boiling liquid will gradually melt the yam lumps, causing the thin broth to transform into a rich, creamy, light-colored soup.
  15. Finish with Herbs
  16. Once the yam is completely dissolved and the soup reaches your desired thickness, reduce the heat to low.
  17. Stir in the thinly sliced utazi leaves (use sparingly, as they are bitter).
  18. Taste for salt and seasoning, adjust if necessary, and simmer for 2 more minutes before turning off the stove.
