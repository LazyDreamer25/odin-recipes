Abacha

Photo by Adimora chidinma / CC BY-SA 4.0

Description

Abacha, popularly known as African Salad, is a traditional Igbo delicacy made from dried, shredded cassava flakes that are rehydrated and tossed in a rich, spicy palm oil paste. It is flavored with fermented oil bean seeds (ugba), local spices, and crayfish, and served cold or slightly warm. The dish is celebrated for its unique textures and vibrant toppings, typically including garden eggs, sliced utazi leaves, onions, and fried fish.

Ingredient

  • Base: 4 cups dried Abacha (shredded cassava flakes)
  • Traditional Mix-in: 1 cup Ugba (ukpaka / fermented oil bean slices)
  • The Sauce: 100ml palm oil, 1 teaspoon powdered potash (akanwu), and 2 tablespoons warm water
  • Flavorings: 2 tablespoons ground crayfish, 1 teaspoon ground dry pepper, 2 seasoning cubes, 1 teaspoon ogiri (locust bean paste), and 2 crushed ehuru seeds (calabash nutmeg)
  • Proteins & Toppings: 1 medium red onion (sliced), 2-3 garden eggs (sliced), a handful of garden egg leaves or utazi leaves (thinly sliced), cooked kpomo (cow skin), and fried or grilled mackerel fish.

Steps

  1. Rehydrate the Cassava
  2. Place the dried Abacha flakes into a large bowl.
  3. Pour lukewarm or hot water over them and soak for 3 to 5 minutes until soft but still firm.
  4. Pour into a colander to drain completely, then set aside.
  5. Create the Palm Oil Emulsion
  6. Dissolve 1 teaspoon of potash in 2 tablespoons of water. Let any residue settle to the bottom.
  7. Pour the palm oil into a clean, dry pot or large bowl off the stove.
  8. Gradually pour in only the clear potash liquid while stirring continuously. The oil will instantly curdle and turn into a thick, bright yellow paste.
  9. Mix the Seasoning Paste
  10. Add the ground crayfish, pepper, crushed ehuru, seasoning cubes, and ogiri paste into the yellow oil mixture.
  11. Stir vigorously until the local spices dissolve completely into the oil.
  12. Mix in the cooked kpomo, ugba slices, and half of your sliced onions.
  13. Combine and Toss
  14. Gradually add the drained Abacha flakes to the pot.
  15. Use a large spoon or clean hands to mix thoroughly until every single strand of cassava is evenly coated in the orange-yellow sauce.
  16. Taste the mixture before adding salt, as the crayfish and seasoning cubes may provide enough flavor.
  17. Heat and Serve
  18. Optional: If you prefer your Abacha warm, place the pot on low heat for 2 to 3 minutes just to warm it through, stirring constantly so it doesn't turn watery.
  19. Dish it out and garnish generously with the remaining sliced onions, garden egg slices, chopped utazi or garden egg leaves, and your fried mackerel.
