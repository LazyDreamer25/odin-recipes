Ekpang

Ekpangnkukwo 01.jpg by Artemisthe1st is licensed underCC BY-SA 4.0

Description

Ekpang Nkukwo is a traditional Efik and Ibibio delicacy made from freshly grated cocoyam and water yam wrapped in tender cocoyam leaves, then slowly simmered in a rich, highly seasoned seafood broth.It is considered a premium Nigerian dish, celebrated for its complex layers of flavor derived from generous amounts of palm oil, periwinkles, crayfish, and local spices like scent leaves.

Ingredient

  • The Base: 3 medium cocoyams (taro) and 1 small slice of water yam (optional, adds softness)
  • The Wrappers: 1 large bunch of fresh, tender cocoyam leaves (substitute with pumpkin leaves/ugu or spinach if unavailable)
  • Seafood: 2 cups shelled or unshelled periwinkles, 1 cup dried prawns, and \(\frac{1}{2}\) cup ground crayfish
  • Proteins: 500g assorted meats (beef, tripe, or kpomo) and 1 medium smoked or dry fish
  • The Sauce Base: 1 cup pure palm oil and 3-4 scotch bonnets (ata rodo), blended or crushed
  • Seasoning & Herbs: 3 seasoning cubes, salt to taste, and a handful of freshly chopped scent leaves (nchanwu/efirin) or uziza leaves

Steps

  1. Prep the Leaves and Proteins
  2. Wash the cocoyam leaves thoroughly. Tear or cut them into medium, palm-sized pieces, removing any tough stalks, and set aside.
  3. Season and boil your assorted meats with onions, pepper, and seasoning cubes until tender. Save the meat broth (stock).
  4. Clean the periwinkles thoroughly. If using unshelled ones, cut off the tails and wash them repeatedly to remove mud. Cut the smoked fish into bite-sized pieces.
  5. Grate and Mix the Yams
  6. Peel the cocoyams and water yam, wash them clean, and grate them using the smallest, finest side of a hand grater to create a smooth, sticky paste.
  7. Mix the grated yams together thoroughly with a pinch of salt. Do not add water; the paste needs to be thick and pliable.
  8. Wrap the Ekpang
  9. Pour half of your palm oil into a large, cold pot and grease the bottom and sides evenly.
  10. Place the periwinkles across the bottom of the pot to create a protective bed so the pudding doesn't burn.
  11. Take a piece of leaf, place a small spoonful of the grated yam paste on it, and spread it slightly along the leaf.
  12. Roll the leaf tightly into a small, thin cylinder, pinching the ends slightly so the yam doesn't squeeze out.
  13. Arrange the rolls carefully in the pot, layering them neatly in a crisscross pattern until all the yam paste is wrapped.4
  14. Cook and Simmer
  15. Pour your boiling meat stock and extra hot water gently down the inner sides of the pot (do not pour directly on top of the rolls, or they will dissolve). The liquid should just submerge the top layer.
  16. Place the pot on medium-high heat. Cover tightly and let it boil vigorously for 15 to 20 minutes without stirring. This allows the yam paste to cook and harden inside the leaves.
  17. Once the wraps are firm, add the cooked meats, smoked fish, dried prawns, ground crayfish, crushed fresh pepper, and the remaining palm oil.
  18. Use a wooden spoon to gently lift from the bottom and stir the mixture together. Reduce the heat to medium-low and let it simmer for another 15 minutes until a thick, rich sauce forms.
  19. Garnish and Serve
  20. Taste for salt and seasoning cubes, adjusting if necessary.
  21. Stir in the chopped scent leaves or uziza leaves and let it steam for 2 more minutes until the aroma is released.
  22. Turn off the heat and allow the dish to rest for 10 minutes to thicken before serving warm.
